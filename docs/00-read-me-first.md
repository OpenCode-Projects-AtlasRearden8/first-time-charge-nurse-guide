# Read This First

**Education only — this field manual is never medical advice.** This manual teaches role-taking, fairness habits, communication scripts, staffing math, and end-of-shift routines for nurses asked to take the charge role for the first time. It does not teach clinical care and never overrides your facility's policies, your manager's direction, your house supervisor, your state's practice act, or your clinical team. If a situation touches patient safety, follow your facility's protocols and escalation chain — that comes before anything written here.

## Who this is for

- Licensed nurses asked to "take charge" or "run the board" for the first time — usually on a unit where you have been a staff nurse for a while.
- Nurses who say yes and then think *wait, what did I just agree to?* That reaction is normal and survivable.
- Float-pool and agency nurses who get voluntold into the desk shift.

It was written in plain English, step by step, the way a friend would explain it — never condescending, no jargon without a translation.

## What makes this different from its siblings

This manual is about the *role*, not the year and not the teaching:

- **new-nurse-first-year-guide** covers surviving your first year as a bedside nurse. Start there if year one is the problem.
- **nurse-preceptor-toolkit-guide** covers being assigned to teach a new hire one-on-one. Read that when you are handed an orientee.
- **This manual** covers what changes when your shift is about the whole unit instead of four or five rooms: the assignment grid, the huddle, the flow math, the phone calls, the escalation reflex, and the debrief at the end.

## How to use it

1. Skim the whole manual once before your first charged shift so nothing feels like a surprise.
2. Charge day: use the [charge-day setup sheet](../worksheets/charge-day-setup.html) in your first hour.
3. Keep the [escalation card](../worksheets/escalation-card.html) and [huddle card](../worksheets/huddle-card.html) in a pocket. When the shift speeds up, you will not have time to remember — you need to read.
4. After each charge shift, run the [debrief sheet](../worksheets/debrief-sheet.html). Ten shifts of debriefs teach you more than any chapter here.

## Ground rules this manual keeps

- **Observed-style examples only.** Every example is a how-it-usually-goes composite from unit life. No names, no rooms, no patient details, ever.
- **Policy wins.** Wherever your facility's paperwork, staffing plan, or chain of command says something different, do that and write down the difference.
- **Nothing personal stays personal.** Charge notes about real people do not belong on personal paper — the worksheets are blank shells on purpose.

## The one-line summary

Charge is not about being the most skilled nurse in the room; it is about being the most *organized* one — the fairness grid, the five-minute huddle, the flow math, the phone voice, and knowing who to call before you need them.
