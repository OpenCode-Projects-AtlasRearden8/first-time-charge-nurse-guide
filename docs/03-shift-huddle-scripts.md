# Shift Huddle Scripts

**Education only — this field manual is never medical advice.** This chapter gives you a five-minute huddle script and the word-level versions of the six sentences that matter. It does not replace any required unit briefing, safety huddle format, or privacy rules — yours wins where they differ.

## Why five minutes matters

A huddle is not a meeting. It is the shift's single cheapest intervention: five minutes that prevent the twelve "I didn't know we were doing that today" failures. Run it within the first half hour — after the grid is built, before walking the floor.

## The basic script (memorize the shape, read the blanks)

Stand, do not sit. Keep the whole group on their feet too — huddles that sit turn into meetings.

```text
[CHARGE HUDDLE — 5 MIN, 6 SENTENCES]

01  "Huddle — five minutes, everyone. Gather up."
02  "Staff: [nurses] and [aids]. Floats: [who]. Watches: [clients/q-checks/things]."
03  "Board: [X] holds are coming, [Y] discharges by [hour], likely [Z] admits —
     I'll keep the desk updated as it changes."
04  "Watch-fors: [call-light pattern / room pairing / weather / equipment /
     particular rooms with planned movement]."
05  "Breaks: your break is promised [when-plan]. Cover each other per the grid —
     the desk covers gaps that become unsafe."
06  "Questions? Anyone who needs something I didn't cover — grab me at the desk.
     Break."
```

Read it, do not recite it. Nobody remembers a freely-composed paragraph, but everyone remembers a read list. Sponsored by the exact same piece of paper as [the huddle card worksheet](../worksheets/huddle-card.html), so it exists in your pocket.

Keep it under five minutes. If somebody starts a question that needs more than one sentence of answer, bump it:

> "Good one — grab me at the desk after and I'll spend real time on it."

## Sentence 4, "Watch-fors" — the one that saves the shift

This is the sentence people remember at 15:00. Watch-fors are any of:

- **Operational risk with a name:** "2 rooms going down for planned maintenance at 10, expect escorts around it."
- **A shape, not a person:** "If call lights bunch up after lunch, that's the discharge wave — look left before you leave your hall."
- **A re-supply gap:** "Supply won't restock readies before noon. Last trip is 11:45."
- **One human beat, if appropriate:** "First shift back for someone — keep it kind."

Never use watch-fors to identify or hint at a person's problem. Watch-fors are about the *floor*, not about anybody.

## The mid-shift micro-huddle

Anytime the board lurches (multiple simultaneous discharges, an unplanned admit burst, a call-out), run the 60-second version at the desk:

```text
[60-SECOND MICRO-HUDDLE]
"Not the day we planned. The board now: [one state].
 What changes for the next two hours: [one change].
 I need one thing from everyone: [one ask]. Go."
```

The discipline is the "one thing." If you want three things, you are running a meeting, not a micro-huddle.

## The end-of-shift thank-down (the huddle's sibling)

Run the inverse huddle in the last ten minutes of the shift, standing, same spot — two sentences, no agenda:

> "Thank you for today — specifically [one thing the team did well]. Hand off per usual and get out of here safe."

Say one specific thing. "Good job" costs nothing and lands nowhere; "everybody covered the discharge wave without me asking" costs three seconds and lands somewhere tomorrow. This is the setup for the longer debrief in [chapter 8](08-end-of-shift-debrief.html).

## When huddles go wrong (and the fix)

| Failure shape | What it looks like | The fix |
|---|---|---|
| The monologue | You said nine things and fielded zero questions | Cut to the 6-sentence script; questions to the desk |
| The vent circle | Huddle becomes a complaint about another unit or person | One sentence: "Park it — see me directly." Then move on |
| The empty one | Nobody showed and you bellowed it over the desk phone | Huddle the next bodies in; never skip it fully |
| The repeat shower | Same announcements every day | Move stale ones to a whiteboard; keep huddle for fresh facts |

## The one-line summary

Six sentences, feet on the floor, five minutes, questions parked at the desk — and sentence 4 (watch-fors) is the one your teammates will still be quoting at 15:00.
