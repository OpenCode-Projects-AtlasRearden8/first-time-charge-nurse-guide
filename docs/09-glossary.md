# Glossary: Charge-Desk Words

**Education only — this field manual is never medical advice.** The charge desk has its own dialect, half staffing-logistics and half unit folklore. Here are the words translated the way a friendly coworker would. Where your facility defines a term differently in policy, your facility's definition wins.

## Role and coverage words

| Word | What people mean by it |
|---|---|
| **Charge (the desk)** | The shift's organizer: assignments, board, huddles, escalations, callback pad. Not a management rank; a shift role. |
| **Grid / assignment sheet** | The written map of who cares for which rooms. Your grid is the fairest document you produce. |
| **Fixed points** | Grid assignments locked first (skill-level matches, planned co-covers, watch-fors). Everything else is built around them. |
| **Co-tenanted rooms** | Two nurse assignments that agreed during grid-building to cover each other's rooms when needed. |
| **Off-sides / on-sides** | The desk work (grid builds, audits, callbacks) vs. room work. Fair grids count both sides out loud. |
| **House supervisor** | After-unit-hours support person — the first stop for red-level staffing and flow calls. Learn their number before you need it. |
| **Float / agency** | Nurses from other units or staffing pools. Predictability, not special treatment, is what makes them useful today. |
| **Voluntold** | Being "asked" to take charge in a way that is not really a question. Chapter 1's three-question confirmation was written for exactly this. |
| **Acceptable-but-tight** | A reworked grid that runs short but safe for a defined window. Always announce it with the count attached. |

## Board and flow words

| Word | What people mean by it |
|---|---|
| **The board / bed board** | The unit's bed/occupancy status display. Policies define what you may or may not edit; the audit reads it. |
| **Openings** | Rooms that will free up this shift (discharges, transfers out). The fuel side of your two-queue race. |
| **Waiters** | People waiting in the building for a bed here. Each has a clock running from *another* shift. |
| **Open-clean** | A room that is physically empty and turned over. The currency your whole unit trades in. |
| **Weighted occupancy** | Census relative to staffed beds, with heavier rooms weighted heavier. Reads true pressure where raw census misleads. |
| **Turn (turnover)** | The housekeeping process that makes an occupied room open-clean. Chargers chase turns like taxes. |
| **Dangling discharge** | A discharge that stalled — orders, ride, or paperwork — and blocks a room. Weekend and holiday specialty. |
| **The lurch** | The moment the board changes shape faster than the grid: a surge, a cluster of admits, simultaneous discharges. Triggers the 60-second micro-huddle. |
| **Audit (mid-morning / 2-hour)** | The four-minute counting ritual: non-written discharges, open-clean, waiters in house. Chapter 4's core drill. |

## Escalation words

| Word | What people mean by it |
|---|---|
| **Green / amber / red** | This manual's three escalation levels, based on *who has to move* (chapter 6). Sort by the trigger, not by your feelings. |
| **Second-attempt rule** | After two tries at an amber bottleneck, it becomes red. Waiting is not a charger's job. |
| **Chain of command** | Your facility's official path from you upward. Chapter 6's three blanks — desk-visible resource, supervisor/manager, after-hours — are your practical version. |
| **Resource move** | Getting the right people to a room. Always charger work. |
| **Clinical move** | Assessment, judgment, orders. Belongs to the bedside team — charged nurses deliver people, clinicians deliver care. |

## Paper and process words

| Word | What people mean by it |
|---|---|
| **Callback pad** | The desk pad of who-was-promised-what-by-when. The loudest trust instrument at the desk. |
| **Thank-down** | The end-of-shift two-sentence thank-you huddle (chapter 8). Specific beats generic, every time. |
| **Debrief (personal)** | The five-minute, four-question, one-change review you run on yourself after each charge shift. |
| **State-pressure-people** | The charge-to-charge handoff shape: where the board is, what is stuck, who you have. With open loops named by name. |
| **Watch-fors** | Huddle sentence 4: the named risks of today's floor, shapes not persons. The sentence teammates remember at 15:00. |
| **Micro-huddle** | The 60-second desk version: one state, one change, one ask. |

## The one-line summary

Half the desk job is vocabulary: the grid, the two queues, the audit, the chain — and your facility's own policy words always outrank any table here.
