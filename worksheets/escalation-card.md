# Escalation Pocket Card

**Education only — this field manual is never medical advice.** Chapter 6's trigger card and escalation sentence on a printable card. The "who/number" blanks get filled from your facility's actual policy and contact sheet each shift — nothing personal belongs here, and no clinical judgment is delegated by this card.

## The three levels (sort by who has to move, not how you feel)

| Level | Shape | Who gets told | Clock |
|---|---|---|---|
| 🟢 Green | Plan absorbs it; fix inside the unit | No one outside | None |
| 🟡 Amber | Plan is bending — order lag, room clock, staffing gap, first missed callback | Owner of the bottleneck, name + time attached | Same hour |
| 🔴 Red | Plan is broken and waiting will hurt something | House supervisor / manager / resource owner — my policy's channels | Now |

**Second-attempt rule: after two tries at an amber, it becomes red. Waiting is not a charger's job.**

## The escalation sentence (same shape every time)

```text
"Charge desk on ______. I have ______ (the count from my audit —
 waiters, stuck rooms, staffing gap by hours).
 I've already tried ______ and ______.
 What I need is ONE thing: ______.
 Can I ask you for that, or take it to the next step?"
```

Saying the count first ☐ · Naming my two attempts ☐ · One ask, one sentence ☐

## Resource move vs. clinical move

- **Resource move — always mine:** getting the right people to a room, now. "Second body's coming — it's ______."
- **Clinical move — bedside team's:** assessment, judgment, orders. The desk delivers people; clinicians deliver care.

## No-fault culture promise (say it once in a huddle)

> "Flag red anytime — that's what I'm for. Amber after two tries. If you need the room covered so you can make a call, I make that call."
